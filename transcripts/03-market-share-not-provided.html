Discrepancies Between FTC Staff and Dr. Brinkerhoff Market-Share Analyses

Executive assessment

Bottom line. The outcome gap is driven principally by market definition, not arithmetic. Under the threshold formulation used in the FTC Staff Analysis, Staff finds structural presumptions in Great Lakes rigid containers and national food-grade corrugated packaging; Dr. Ansel Brinkerhoff’s analysis, as characterized in the attached materials, finds no presumptively unlawful market. The defense’s greatest present vulnerabilities are (i) the complete omission of food-grade corrugated packaging, (ii) use of a twelve-state rigid-container geography that adds $1.2 billion of sales but only about $140 million of merging-party sales, and (iii) attribution of all $140 million of Brentwood Foods revenue to CPS even though CPS manufactures about 60% and invoices approximately $84 million in tolling fees.

The FTC analysis is not invulnerable. Staff must substantiate the product and geographic boundaries with evidence of actual substitution, not merely regulatory labels or shipment flows; explain its freight imputations; demonstrate consistent removal of intermediate/tolling revenue to avoid double counting; and disclose enough firm-level data to reproduce its HHI calculations. The immediate objective should be an auditable, market-by-market data bridge and a supplemental expert analysis that squarely addresses Markets 3 and 4.

Highest substantive risk: Market 4, because the FTC reports a 2,210 post-merger HHI and 360-point delta and the parties currently have no responsive market analysis.

Highest boundary dispute: Market 3, where geography alone moves the combined share from 32.9% to 40.0% and the approximate delta from 537 to 798.

Highest credibility risk: Market 2, where the expert’s full-$140 million CPS attribution conflicts with the contract economics and leaves an unexplained approximately $10 million combined-revenue gap.

Highest data-comparability risk: Market 1, where product scope, freight basis, reporting period, and HHI treatment all change simultaneously.

Scope and important source limitation

This memorandum compares (1) the FTC Staff Economic Analysis dated April 2, 2025 and (2) the Brinkerhoff figures and methodology described in that analysis and in the Tolling Agreement Summary Memorandum dated April 7, 2025. The latter is counsel’s summary of the Brentwood arrangement—not Dr. Brinkerhoff’s March 14, 2025 report itself. The original expert report, underlying spreadsheets, Freedonia extracts, subpoena datasets, and CPS sales bridge were not included in the materials reviewed. Accordingly, statements about the expert’s analysis are limited to the figures and descriptions in the two attached documents; any characterization should be verified against the original report and workpapers before use in a filing, declaration, or examination.
